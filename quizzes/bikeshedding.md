# Quiz: Bikeshedding

## A design review spends 40 minutes on endpoint naming and none on payment timeouts. What helps next time?

- [x] Open with the decisions needed and delegate naming to a style guide.
- [ ] Ban all discussion of names in reviews.
- [ ] Extend the review to two hours.
- [ ] Invite more people to the review.

**Why:** Point reviewers at the risky part and automate or delegate matters of taste. See [[bikeshedding]].

## Why do small decisions attract the most debate?

- [x] Everyone understands them well enough to have an opinion; big ones take effort to understand.
- [ ] Small decisions are usually the most important ones.
- [ ] Big decisions are always made in advance by managers.
- [ ] Small decisions are harder to reverse.

**Why:** It's Parkinson's law of triviality: the nuclear plant passes in minutes; the bike shed takes 45. See [[bikeshedding]].

## When is a long debate about a name worth it?

- [x] When it's in a public API or becomes the domain's shared vocabulary.
- [ ] When it's a local variable in a private function.
- [ ] When the team has already decided it once.
- [ ] Never; names are always bike sheds.

**Why:** Long-lived names are worth getting right. Calling everything a bike shed is a listed trap. See [[bikeshedding]].
