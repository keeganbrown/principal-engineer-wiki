# Quiz: Technical Strategy

## A draft strategy says "We will become cloud-native and world-class at reliability by 2028." What is it missing?

- [x] A diagnosis of what's actually wrong, and actions that follow from it.
- [ ] A more ambitious date, so that teams feel the urgency of the goal.
- [ ] More goals, so that every team can see its own work reflected in it.
- [ ] Nothing; a clear aspiration is all a technical strategy really needs.

**Why:** A goal dressed up as a plan tells nobody what to do differently. A strategy needs a specific diagnosis, a guiding policy that rules options out, and a few coherent actions. See [[technical-strategy]].

## Which guiding policy will actually guide teams?

- [x] "New services use our one supported database engine."
- [ ] "Each team should pick the right tool for each job."
- [ ] "We value simplicity, reliability, and developer speed."
- [ ] "We will adopt modern technology wherever it makes sense."

**Why:** A good guiding policy rules options out. "The right tool for each job" excludes nothing and guides nobody. See [[technical-strategy]].

## A principal engineer writes a strong strategy alone and publishes it as a surprise, with no funding for its actions. What is likely to happen?

- [x] Teams ignore it, because they weren't involved and nobody staffed the work.
- [ ] Teams adopt it quickly, since a strong strategy speaks for itself.
- [ ] Leadership funds it automatically once it's published to everyone.
- [ ] It succeeds, provided the document is long enough to cover every case.

**Why:** Build it with the teams it affects and get a sponsor who will fund the actions. A strategy without staffing is a wish, and one written in isolation gets ignored. See [[technical-strategy]] and [[influencing-without-authority]].

## Two teams of engineers who talk every day want a formal technical strategy document. Do they need one?

- [x] Probably not; a handful of decision records is enough at that size.
- [ ] Yes; every organization needs a strategy document before it grows.
- [ ] Yes, and it should be at least twenty pages to be taken seriously.
- [ ] No; strategy is a job for executives, never for engineers at all.

**Why:** One or two teams who talk daily don't need a strategy document; a few architecture decision records will do. See [[technical-strategy]].
