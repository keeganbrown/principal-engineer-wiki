# Wiki review: quizzes, 2026-11-01

Scope: all 66 quizzes (198 questions), each read against its article as it
reads now. Article prose was out of scope unless a quiz exposed a problem in
it. Reviewers: **NR** newcomer-reader · **TA** technical-accuracy · **PS**
principal-skeptic · **SE** style-editor · **WG** wiki-gardener (whole-wiki
section at the end). The per-article reviewers ran in four batches of 16–17.

When several reviewers flagged the same thing, their tags appear together
(e.g. `[TA PS NR]`). Agreement is a sign of priority.

## At a glance

| Severity | Count |

| should-fix | 91 |

No marked answer is simply wrong. Every reviewer agreed that the answer keys
match the articles. The must-fix items are a sentence that says the opposite of
what it means, two questions with two defensible answers, a Why line that
contradicts another article, and a question that breaks when shuffled.

### Must-fix

1. **expand-and-contract** · "When can you skip the full dance?" The Why line says "Additive changes don't need it. **So does** a change where you control every reader…", which literally means the second kind of change *does* need the full dance. → "…and neither does a change where…" `[SE TA NR PS WG]`
2. **contract-testing** · "What can contract tests not tell you?" The wrong option "Which consumers depend on a given field" is defensible: the article says only consumers that publish contracts are counted, so you confirm with usage metrics. → Replace it with something contracts clearly catch, e.g. "Whether a field the consumer requires is missing." `[TA PS]`
3. **spec-first-agent-tasks** · "An agent adds an in-memory rate-limit counter… What would have caught it?" In the article's example, review *did* catch it, so the "wrong" option "fixing it in review" is true. → "What would have caught it before any code was written?" `[PS must-fix, TA should-fix, SE]`
4. **api-versioning** · float → integer cents. The Why says "A type change can't be made additively, so it needs an explicit version." The article says "under the same name", and its own rule (and [[expand-and-contract]]) is to add a new field. → Restore the qualifier and say why `/v2` was chosen here. This also exposes an article gap: the Example never says why a new field wasn't enough. `[PS]`
5. **kiss** · "The same team already runs a job queue… What changes?" The question only makes sense after the nightly-report question, and the site shuffles questions. It is also the only hedged option ("may now be") among absolutes. → Make it self-contained. `[SE must-fix, NR should-fix, WG]`

### Cross-cutting themes (in priority order)

1. **The longest option is the answer.** Measured over all 198 questions: the correct option is the longest in **171 (86%)**. Chance is about 25%. On average it is 1.45× the length of a wrong option. Always picking the longest scores about 84% without reading. By category: Leadership 100%, Coding with AI 93%, Pitfalls 93%, Technology 90%, Architecture 79%, Practices 76%, Principles 76%. In 46 of 66 quizzes it holds for every question. Only `feature-flags` never does; copy it. The cause is usually that only the right option carries a "; reason" / "so…" clause. → Move reasons into **Why:** or give every distractor its own plausible reason clause. Consider a non-blocking lint warning when the answer is more than ~1.3× the average distractor. `[all five]`
2. **Hedges mark the right answer and absolutes mark the wrong ones.** Correct options hedge ("may/often/usually") twice as often as distractors. All 13 options that start with "Whenever" are wrong. Verdict openers ("Never;", "Yes;", "Nothing;") are wrong 26 times and right 3 times. → Make some absolutes correct where the article is absolute ("never fail open", "never reuse a field"), and hedge some distractors. `[WG NR SE]`
3. **Throwaway distractors.** "Lowest priority in the company", "ten entries", "forever", "editor theme", "fewer than one team", "the person who typed it up". → Use the article's own Common traps as near-miss distractors, as backpressure, circuit-breaker and not-invented-here already do. `[NR SE]`
4. **Why lines keep the rule and drop the article's cost or caveat.** Examples: jitter vs a hot key, RAG "fixes" it (7% stay wrong), no break-glass in IaC, "default to one strong store" (CAP), "risk, not interest" (debt), "allowlist narrows, doesn't close" (trifecta), shadow mode on write paths, rejection hands waiting to the client (backpressure). → Add one clause each. `[PS]`
5. **"When it doesn't apply" is almost never tested.** Quizzes reward knowing when to use a practice, not when to skip it: hero-culture, hexagonal, event-driven, circuit-breaker, spec-first, message-brokers, paved-roads, feature-flags, saga isolation. → Aim for at least one "not yet / it depends" question per quiz. `[PS]`
6. **Questions that depend on context shuffling removes.** About 13 questions lean on an earlier question or on the article's example: kiss, coupling-and-cohesion, cqrs, god-object, hexagonal ("What's the main testing benefit?" has no subject), infrastructure-as-code, modular-monolith, not-invented-here, expand-and-contract, strangler-fig, big-bang-rewrite, distributed-monolith, api-styles, plus blameless-postmortems, contract-testing, design-docs, object-storage. → Name the pattern and setup in each question. `[WG SE NR]`
7. **Recall instead of judgment.** "What did the team do/choose?", "What fixed it?", "How did the reviewer catch it?", "What does the wiki suggest?", "Which is a listed trap?" (about 15 questions). → "What should they do?" usually converts them with no other change. `[all]`
8. **"…is a listed trap" in 29 Why lines.** Seen in the cross-wiki Quiz tab, it doesn't say where the trap is listed, and it justifies by citation. → Give the one-clause reason instead. `[SE NR WG]`
9. **Jargon the article doesn't define** used in quizzes: temperature, TTL (in non-caching quizzes), Square/Rectangle, ship dark, ORM, chatty, seams, at-least-once, hooks (agent-instruction-files), event sourcing (DDD), and unexpanded ADR, PR, SLO, CRUD, MCP. `[NR SE]`
